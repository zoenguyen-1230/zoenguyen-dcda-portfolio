About Me

Who I am: Hi, I’m Zoe Nguyen, a senior majoring in Strategic Communication with a minor in Digital Culture & Data Analytics and Communication Studies.

I approach the internet like a cultural field site.

I’m interested in how people construct identity, belonging, and meaning in digital spaces through comments, trends, aesthetics, and algorithms. I see platforms not just as tools, but as environments that shape behavior, norms, and social rituals.

Whether I’m analyzing engagement data, mapping digital patterns, or observing how a TikTok sound travels across communities, I’m drawn to the question: What does this reveal about us?

Outside the classroom, I document cities through cafe reviews, rank airports with surprising seriousness, and archive everyday moments like artifacts.

My interests: My interests usually start with something small. A TikTok trend that feels bigger than it should. A comment section that turns into a community. A cafe that becomes a personality trait. I’m drawn to the everyday rituals of digital life, the way we curate playlists for cities we haven’t moved to yet, rank airlines like they’re brand case studies, or build entire identities through aesthetics and captions.

I’m especially curious about how algorithms shape visibility and belonging: who gets seen, who goes viral, and how certain narratives travel across platforms. I think about how digital spaces function like modern cultural ecosystems, where meaning is constantly negotiated through trends, edits, memes, and metadata.

A lot of my “random” interests feed this curiosity. Ranking airports. Reviewing cafés. Tracking airline loyalty programs. Screenshotting good copy. Documenting travel like I’m building a personal archive. They may seem unrelated, but they’re all ways of studying how environments, physical and digital, shape behavior and perception.

Ultimately, I’m interested in using data and analysis to surface patterns that are already there and study how they influence how we connect, consume, and construct identity online.

What I hope to learn: Through this course, I hope to deepen my ability to translate cultural observation into technical execution. I want to strengthen my skills in Python, data visualization, mapping, and web-based storytelling, not just as technical tools, but as methods for uncovering patterns in digital behavior. My goal is to feel confident moving from insight to analysis to presentation, building work that is both grounded in analysis and creativity. Ultimately, I want to create digital projects that don’t just display data, but interpret and turn raw information into stories about how people live, connect, and construct meaning online.

Semester Reflection

What I learned: Over the course of this semester, I learned much more than just how to build a website. I started by setting up my GitHub portfolio and understanding the basic structure of HTML and CSS, but that foundation expanded into working with data visualization, AI-assisted coding, Tableau dashboards, Python mapping, and Mapbox style design. I learned how design decisions, including color palettes, typography, layout, icon density, shape how information is interpreted. Through Tableau, I practiced choosing meaningful metrics and dimensions to tell a data story. Through Python and APIs, I learned how data flows from a CSV file into an interactive map. I also gained hands-on experience using AI as a coding collaborator, which taught me how to prompt clearly, debug responsibly, and evaluate generated code instead of just copying it. This semester helped me see digital culture not just as content or social media, but as the systems and structures behind how digital experiences are built.

Challenges I overcame: There were many technical moments that initially felt overwhelming. Early in the semester, I struggled with CSS changes not appearing on my live GitHub site, which encouraged me to learn about file paths, caching, and the importance of committing and pushing correctly. Later, working with Tableau required me to think more critically about sorting, filtering, and layering data in ways that made sense visually. Python mapping presented another challenge, especially formatting Mapbox tile URLs, handling API requests, and debugging geocoding errors. Designing my custom Mapbox basemap also required repeated adjustments to avoid visual clutter and maintain readability across zoom levels. Each challenge required trial and error, careful testing, and patience. Instead of feeling discouraged, I gradually became more comfortable navigating technical problems and breaking them down step by step.

How I grew: Throughout the semester, my understanding of digital culture became more concrete and applied. Before this class, I mostly thought of digital culture in terms of social media, branding, and storytelling. Now, I see it as something that also includes infrastructure: how websites are built, how maps are layered, how data is structured, and how design decisions influence interpretation. I also grew in my confidence with data analytics. While coding in Python still feels challenging at times, especially when working with data cleaning, I now better understand the logic behind how data moves through a system. I may not feel fully fluent in writing code from scratch, but I understand the process more clearly and feel less intimidated by technical tools. That shift from avoidance to curiosity feels like real growth.

Looking ahead: I plan to apply these skills in both creative and professional settings. Building my portfolio taught me how to present work intentionally rather than simply submitting assignments. The visualization and mapping projects showed me how data can be transformed into narrative-driven, interactive experiences. As I move forward in strategic communication and digital media, I can see myself using these skills to build campaign dashboards, interactive storytelling pieces, or branded digital platforms. This semester showed me that technical tools and creativity are not separate but they can strengthen each other. That integration is something I want to continue developing.
